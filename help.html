Drawing Grid — Quick Start Guide

Drawing Grid overlays a customizable grid on any reference photo, with tools to simplify it to black-and-white and to rotate or zoom in — nothing to buy, nothing to install from a store.

Getting Started

  1. Click the link you were sent. It opens right in your web browser (Chrome or Edge) — there is nothing to download or install from an app store.
  2. Optional, but nice to have: pin it so it opens like its own program instead of a browser tab, with no address bar.

    On a computer (Chrome or Edge):

    • Click the three dots in the top-right corner of the browser window.
    • Look for "Save and Share" or "More Tools", then "Create shortcut..."
    • Check the box that says "Open as window", then click Create.
    • Now you'll have an icon on your Desktop or Start Menu that opens the tool by itself, without the rest of the browser around it.

    On an iPhone or iPad (any browser — Chrome, Safari, etc.):

    • Tap the Share icon (the square with an arrow pointing up).
    • Scroll down and tap "Add to Home Screen".
    • Tap Add. You'll get an icon on your Home Screen that opens the tool full-screen, with no address bar or browser tabs around it.

    On an Android phone or tablet:

    • Tap the three dots in the top-right corner of the browser.
    • Tap "Add to Home screen" (Chrome may also offer this as a banner or an "Install app" option), then confirm.

Loading a Photo

  1. Click the blue Choose Photo button.
  2. Pick any photo from your computer, the same way you'd attach a photo to an email. On a phone or tablet, this opens your Photo Library instead, with the option to take a new picture with the camera.
  3. The photo appears full-screen right away, with the default grid already on it.

Your grid, color, black & white, and rotation settings are remembered automatically between visits, as long as the Remember this setup switch (under the Photo tab) is on — it is by default. The photo itself is never remembered, even with that switch on, so you'll pick your photo again each time you reopen the tool or reload the page.

Black & White

Tap the B&W tab at the bottom to open these controls.

  • The Black & White switch turns the photo grayscale — many shades of gray, similar to a black-and-white filter in Photos.
  • The Threshold switch is off by default. Turn it on for a completely different, more graphic look: every pixel becomes pure black or pure white, nothing in between — useful for seeing just the big light and dark shapes of your subject.
    • Once Threshold is on, the Threshold Level slider controls how much of the photo becomes black versus white.
    • Slide it left: only the very darkest shadows turn black, everything else stays white.
    • Slide it right: more of the photo turns black, leaving only the brightest highlights white.

There's no "correct" setting for either — try both and use whichever reads most clearly for what you're drawing.

Rotating the Photo

Tap the Rotate tab at the bottom.

  • 90° Left / 90° Right — turns the photo a quarter-turn at a time. Good for photos taken sideways.
  • Fine Adjust slider — small tilt adjustments, for straightening a photo that's just slightly crooked.
  • Reset Rotation — puts the photo back to how it started.

Adding Grid Lines

Tap the Grid 1 tab at the bottom. There's a second, independent grid under Grid 2 — handy if you want a coarse grid and a fine grid on the photo at the same time.

For each grid you can set:

ControlWhat it does
Show GridTurns that grid on or off
Rows / ColumnsUse the − and + buttons to set how many squares
ColorTap a colored circle to pick the line color
Line WidthHow thick the grid lines are
OpacityHow solid or see-through the lines are

Change any of these and the grid updates on the photo immediately, so you can see the effect as you adjust.

Zooming In and Moving Around

  • To zoom in or out: scroll your mouse wheel (or two-finger scroll on a trackpad) while your cursor is over the photo.
  • To move around once zoomed in: click and drag the photo, the same way you'd drag a picture in any program.
  • To go back to normal size: scroll back out, or reload the page.

On a touchscreen device, pinch with two fingers to zoom, and drag with one finger to move around.

Saving Your Work

Tap the Photo tab, then tap Save. This saves exactly what you're looking at — photo, grid, and black & white all together — as a flattened picture file in your Downloads folder. It's a snapshot for printing or reference, not an editable file — it won't reopen with the grid still adjustable.

Your grid and color settings are saved separately and automatically, any time the Remember this setup switch is on — you don't need to tap Save for that.

If Something Seems Stuck

If a button or slider stops responding, the fastest fix is almost always: reload the page. Just refresh the browser (or close and reopen the app icon, if you pinned it). Your grid and color settings will still be there — you'll just need to pick your photo again.
